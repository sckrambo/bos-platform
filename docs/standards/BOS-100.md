# BOS-100: Core Judgment Architecture

**Status:** Foundation Draft  
**Category:** Core Standard  
**Authority:** Normative  
**Version:** 0.1.0

## 1. Purpose

BOS-100 defines the core architecture of structured judgment within the Business Operating System.

It establishes the primary objects, lifecycle stages, relationships, invariants, and behavioral rules that all conforming BOS implementations must preserve.

This standard is intended to be understandable by humans, implementable by software systems, and testable through conformance rules.

## 2. Scope

BOS-100 governs:

- Judgment Objects
- The Judgment Lifecycle
- Thinking Blocks
- Participants
- Relationships
- Conversations
- Decisions
- Outcomes
- Reflections
- Lessons
- Provenance
- History
- Decision lineage
- Core state transitions

BOS-100 does not define:

- User interface design
- Database technology
- Programming language
- Deployment architecture
- Vendor-specific AI behavior
- Domain-specific judgment rules

Those concerns may be defined by other BOS standards or implementation documents.

## 3. Normative Language

The key words **MUST**, **MUST NOT**, **REQUIRED**, **SHOULD**, **SHOULD NOT**, and **MAY** are to be interpreted as normative requirements.

- **MUST** indicates an absolute requirement.
- **MUST NOT** indicates an absolute prohibition.
- **SHOULD** indicates a strong recommendation that may be departed from only with documented justification.
- **SHOULD NOT** indicates a discouraged practice that may be used only with documented justification.
- **MAY** indicates a permitted implementation choice.

## 4. Core Principle

A consequential judgment MUST preserve sufficient structure to explain:

- What question was being considered
- What was known at the time
- What was assumed
- What alternatives were considered
- What risks were recognized
- Who participated
- What was decided
- What happened afterward
- What was learned

No meaningful act of reasoning shall be lost.

## 5. Judgment Object

The Judgment Object is the primary aggregate within BOS.

A Judgment Object represents a bounded process of structured reasoning concerning a question, issue, opportunity, or decision.

A Judgment Object MUST have:

- A unique identifier
- A title or question
- A lifecycle state
- A creation timestamp
- An originating Participant
- A provenance record
- A history of meaningful changes

A Judgment Object MAY contain:

- Context
- Observations
- Evidence
- Assumptions
- Alternatives
- Risks
- Opportunities
- Constraints
- Questions
- Hypotheses
- Conversations
- Challenges
- Decisions
- Outcomes
- Reflections
- Lessons
- Relationships
- Participants
- Confidence assessments
- Dissent

A Judgment Object is the digital twin of thought.

## 6. Judgment Lifecycle

The canonical BOS Judgment Lifecycle is:

Context

↓

Perception

↓

Judgment

↓

Decision

↓

Outcome

↓

Reflection

Each stage has a distinct responsibility.

### 6.1 Context

Context defines the environment in which the Judgment Object exists.

Context MAY include:

- Organizational conditions
- Historical information
- Constraints
- Objectives
- Stakeholders
- Time boundaries
- External conditions
- Known dependencies

Context MUST describe the environment without presenting interpretation as fact.

### 6.2 Perception

Perception records what Participants observe, receive, measure, or identify.

Perception MAY include:

- Observations
- Evidence
- Questions
- Signals
- Reported facts
- Measurements
- External inputs

Perception MUST distinguish direct observation from interpretation.

### 6.3 Judgment

Judgment transforms context and perception into structured reasoning.

Judgment MAY include:

- Assumptions
- Hypotheses
- Alternatives
- Risks
- Opportunities
- Constraints
- Challenges
- Confidence
- Interpretation

Judgment MUST preserve uncertainty.

Judgment MUST NOT present unsupported inference as established fact.

### 6.4 Decision

Decision records commitment.

A Decision MUST:

- Have a unique identifier
- Be attributable to one or more responsible Participants
- Have a timestamp
- Preserve its lineage
- Record the alternatives considered when known
- Preserve dissent when present
- Preserve confidence or uncertainty when assessed

A Decision MUST NOT silently overwrite an earlier Decision.

A revised Decision MUST be represented as a new Decision or an explicit superseding event.

### 6.5 Outcome

Outcome records what occurred after a Decision or meaningful action.

An Outcome MUST remain distinct from the Decision that preceded it.

An Outcome MAY include:

- Results
- Measurements
- Consequences
- Variance from expectation
- Unintended effects
- New evidence

A favorable Outcome does not prove that the original Judgment was sound.

An unfavorable Outcome does not prove that the original Judgment was unsound.

### 6.6 Reflection

Reflection records learning after Outcomes are known.

Reflection MAY include:

- What was understood correctly
- What was missed
- Which assumptions failed
- Which risks materialized
- Which signals were ignored
- What should change in future judgments

Reflection MUST NOT rewrite the historical record.

Reflection MUST preserve the distinction between:

- What was known at the time
- What became known afterward

## 7. Thinking Blocks

A Thinking Block is a structured object representing one meaningful cognitive concept.

Every Thinking Block MUST have:

- A unique identifier
- A type
- Content
- An originating Participant
- A creation timestamp
- Provenance
- A parent Judgment Object
- A history of meaningful changes

A Thinking Block SHOULD contain one primary cognitive concept.

Thinking Blocks MUST NOT be reduced to untyped text when their meaning is known.

### 7.1 Core Thinking Block Types

The core Thinking Block types are:

- Observation
- Evidence
- Assumption
- Alternative
- Risk
- Opportunity
- Constraint
- Question
- Hypothesis
- Decision
- Outcome
- Reflection
- Lesson

Implementations MAY define additional types, provided they do not weaken the meaning of the core types.

## 8. Participants

A Participant is any identifiable actor that contributes to a Judgment Object.

Participant types MAY include:

- Human
- Artificial Intelligence
- Department
- Organization
- External Advisor
- Connected System

Every meaningful contribution MUST identify its originating Participant.

A Participant record SHOULD preserve:

- Identity
- Participant type
- Role
- Permissions
- Relevant expertise
- Organizational affiliation
- Trust or validation status when applicable

AI Participants MUST be distinguishable from human Participants.

## 9. Relationships

A Relationship connects two identifiable BOS objects.

Every Relationship MUST have:

- A unique identifier
- A source object
- A target object
- A relationship type
- An originating Participant or system
- A creation timestamp
- Provenance

Relationship types MAY include:

- Supports
- Challenges
- Contradicts
- Informs
- Depends On
- Derived From
- Supersedes
- Relates To
- Produced
- Resulted In
- Refines
- Validates
- Invalidates

Relationships MUST preserve direction when direction is meaningful.

Implementations MUST NOT infer irreversible meaning from an ambiguous relationship.

## 10. Conversations

A Conversation is a structured exchange attached to a meaningful BOS object or relationship.

A Conversation MUST attach to at least one of the following:

- A Thinking Block
- A Relationship
- A Decision
- An Outcome
- A Reflection
- A Judgment Object

A Conversation MUST preserve:

- Participants
- Contributions
- Timing
- Attachment context
- Provenance

A Conversation MUST NOT exist as context-free global discussion within the core Judgment model.

A Conversation MAY produce a promoted Thinking Block.

Promotion MUST preserve the originating Conversation and provenance.

## 11. Decision Lineage

Decision lineage is the traceable reasoning path that explains how a Decision came to be.

A Decision MUST be traceable to the relevant reasoning available at the time.

Decision lineage MAY include:

- Context
- Observations
- Evidence
- Assumptions
- Alternatives
- Risks
- Opportunities
- Constraints
- Challenges
- Conversations
- Participants
- Confidence
- Dissent

The absence of Evidence MUST be representable.

The absence of consensus MUST be representable.

The presence of unresolved uncertainty MUST be representable.

## 12. Provenance

Provenance records the origin and history of a meaningful object or contribution.

Provenance MUST preserve, when applicable:

- Originating Participant
- Creation time
- Source
- Parent object
- Prior version
- Transformation history
- AI involvement
- External-system involvement

Provenance MUST NOT be silently removed.

## 13. Historical Integrity

BOS history is append-oriented.

Meaningful historical records MUST NOT be silently overwritten or deleted.

Corrections MUST be represented transparently.

Revisions SHOULD create:

- A new version
- A superseding object
- A correction event
- An explicit amendment

Reflection MUST NOT alter the content of the original Decision.

## 14. State Transitions

A Judgment Object MAY move through lifecycle stages nonlinearly.

Implementations MAY allow:

- Returning from Judgment to Perception
- Adding Context after initial creation
- Reopening a prior Decision
- Recording multiple Outcomes
- Recording multiple Reflections
- Suspending and resuming work

Implementations MUST preserve the order in which events occurred.

A later lifecycle event MUST NOT be represented as having existed earlier than it did.

## 15. Core Invariants

A conforming BOS implementation MUST preserve the following invariants:

1. Every Judgment Object has identity.
2. Every meaningful contribution has attribution.
3. Every Decision has lineage.
4. Reflection does not rewrite history.
5. Conversations attach to meaningful context.
6. Thinking Blocks retain type.
7. AI contributions remain identifiable.
8. Dissent may be preserved.
9. Uncertainty may be represented.
10. History remains replayable.
11. Relationships retain provenance.
12. Outcomes remain distinct from Decisions.

## 16. Replay

A conforming implementation SHOULD support replay of a Judgment Object.

Replay SHOULD allow a reviewer to understand:

- What existed at a particular time
- What Participants knew
- What assumptions were active
- What alternatives were under consideration
- What Decision was made
- What information arrived afterward
- How Reflection changed future understanding

Replay MUST NOT use later knowledge to falsely alter the historical state.

## 17. Extension Rules

Implementations MAY extend BOS-100 with:

- Domain-specific Thinking Blocks
- Additional relationship types
- Specialized Participant roles
- Domain-specific validation
- Additional lifecycle states
- Automated analysis

Extensions MUST NOT:

- Remove provenance
- Collapse Decision and Outcome
- Merge Reflection into Decision history
- Hide AI participation
- Eliminate lineage
- Prevent dissent from being represented
- Weaken historical integrity

## 18. Minimum Conformance

A minimally conforming implementation of BOS-100 MUST support:

- Creation of a Judgment Object
- Creation of typed Thinking Blocks
- Participant attribution
- Explicit Relationships
- Decision lineage
- Outcome recording
- Reflection recording
- Provenance
- Historical replay
- Prevention of silent historical overwrite

## 19. Example

A health system is considering whether to launch a new oncology service.

The Judgment Object records:

- Market Context
- Patient-volume Observations
- Financial Evidence
- Staffing Assumptions
- Build, partner, and defer Alternatives
- Regulatory Risks
- Participant Challenges
- A Decision to begin a pilot
- Confidence at the time of Decision
- Six-month Outcomes
- Reflection on which assumptions were incorrect
- Lessons linked to future service-line judgments

The later Reflection does not change the original Decision.

It adds learning to the historical record.

## 20. Conformance Intent

BOS-100 defines behavior rather than implementation technology.

A conforming implementation may use any:

- Programming language
- Database
- User interface
- Deployment model
- AI model
- Infrastructure

Conformance depends on preserving the architecture and invariants defined by this standard.
